The Broken Umbrella

🎈 Ages 4-8⭐ Honesty🏡 Family

⏱ 3 min readहिन्दी में पढ़ें

Dadi's umbrella was old, older than Meera, maybe even older than Meera's mother. It was big and blue, with a curved wooden handle painted bright blue too, and it had kept Dadi dry through more monsoons than anyone could count.

Meera loved it. She liked to open it inside the house and pretend it was a sky, or a tent, or a magic flying machine.

That afternoon, it was a flying machine. Meera held it high, spun round and round, and jumped off the steel trunk.

Snap.

Meera landed. She looked up. One of the umbrella's thin metal ribs was bent the wrong way, and a piece of blue cloth flapped loose like a broken wing.

"Oh no..." Meera whispered. Her heart started to pound. "Grandma's umbrella is broken! What do I do now?"

Right then, from the next room, she heard Dadi's voice. "Meera, dear! The clouds are gathering. Could you bring me my umbrella?"

Meera froze. Quickly, without thinking, she pushed the umbrella behind her back.

Dadi came in, wiping her hands on her dupatta. Outside the window, the sky had turned dark grey.

"The umbrella?" Meera's voice came out all squeaky. "I... I just can't find it anywhere, Grandma."

The words felt heavy as soon as they were out. Like stones in her pocket.

Dadi looked at her for a long moment. Then she tilted her head. "Really?" she said gently. "But its blue handle is peeking out from behind you."

Meera looked down. There it was: the curved blue handle, sticking out right beside her knee. She had hidden the umbrella, but not very well.

Her face went hot. Her eyes went prickly. And then, all at once, the truth came tumbling out.

"Grandma... it was my fault. I was playing with it and I jumped and it broke. I'm sorry. I was scared you'd be angry."

She waited for Dadi to be cross.

But Dadi just knelt down, took the umbrella, and looked at the bent rib. Then she looked at Meera, and her eyes were soft.

"The umbrella can be fixed, child," she said. "A little wire, a few stitches. But hiding the truth? That makes the heart heavy. Didn't it feel heavy just now?"

Meera nodded. It had. It really had.

Dadi tucked a curl behind Meera's ear. "You told the truth. And that matters most of all."

And just like that, the heavy stones were gone. Meera felt so light she could have floated up to the ceiling without any umbrella at all.

She wiped her eyes and grinned. "Then come on, Grandma, let's fix it together!"

So they sat on the floor while the first raindrops pattered on the roof, Dadi with her needle and thread, Meera holding the wire straight. And when the big blue umbrella opened again, it was even more special than before, because now it held a story too.

⭐ The lesson

Telling the truth makes the heart feel light.

💭 Think about it

Why was Meera scared to tell the truth? And how did she feel after she did?
