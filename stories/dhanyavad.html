Saying Thank You

⏱ 4 min readहिन्दी में पढ़ें

Dadi's courtyard was the best place in the whole village. There was a neem tree for shade, a charpai for naps, a tulsi plant in the middle, and, if you were lucky, a steel tin full of laddoos.

Golu came here to play every day. Golu was fast, Golu was funny, and Golu was always in a hurry.

"Dadi, give me a laddoo! Quick!" he shouted one afternoon, running in. He grabbed one from the tin and was out of the gate before Dadi could even blink.

No please. No thank you. Not even a smile.

A minute later he was yelling across the courtyard, "Chhotu, throw the ball here! Now!"

Dadi watched him over her glasses. Then she called Meera and Chhotu over and spoke very softly.

"Let's play a little game," she whispered. "Tomorrow, we won't say please or thank you to Golu either. Just for one day." She raised one finger. "But gently, my dears. Nobody makes fun of him. Agreed?"

Meera and Chhotu looked at each other and nodded. A secret game! This would be interesting.

The next day, Dadi's game began.

Golu was in the middle of a lovely throw when Chhotu held out his hands. "Golu, give me the ball!" Golu tossed it over. Chhotu caught it and simply walked off.

Golu stood there with his mouth open. "Hey! You didn't even say thanks?"

A little later, Golu wanted to do something nice. He filled a glass with cool water from the pot and carried it carefully to Meera, without spilling a single drop. "Meera, here's some water. I got it myself!"

Meera barely looked up. "Hmm. Just put it there."

That was it. Golu put the glass down slowly. Something inside him felt small and scratchy, like a pebble in a shoe.

He went and sat by Dadi on the charpai. "Dadi," he said in a quiet voice, "everyone's acting funny today. Nobody says thank you to me."

Dadi put her arm around him. "And how did that feel, Golu?"

Golu thought about it. "Bad," he said. "Like... like nobody even cares about me."

Dadi nodded slowly. "Sweetheart, you don't say please or thank you to anyone either."

Golu went very still. He thought about the laddoo he'd grabbed. About shouting at Chhotu. And then his eyes went round. "Oh! So that's what everyone was showing me!"

Chhotu came running up, looking a bit guilty. "Sorry, Golu, it was Dadi's game! And for the ball yesterday..." He grinned. "Thank you!"

Golu laughed, and the scratchy pebble feeling melted away. He turned to Dadi and put his hands together. "Dadi, may I have a laddoo, please?" She gave him one. "Thank you, Dadi!"

Dadi's whole face crinkled into a smile. "See? Two little words, and my heart feels happy."

Golu held out the tin. "Meera, Chhotu, please have a laddoo!"

"Thank you, Golu!" said Meera. "You're so sweet."

Then Golu looked up at the big neem tree, its leaves rustling above him, and shouted, "Thank you, neem tree, for the shade!"

Chhotu nearly fell over laughing. "Ha ha! You're thanking the tree too?"

"Why not?" said Golu, grinning. "It works hard all day!"

And from that day on, Golu always said please and thank you: to his friends, to Dadi, to the shopkeeper, and sometimes, just for fun, to the neem tree. And wherever he went, people smiled back.

⭐ The lesson

Little words like please and thank you tell people that we care about them.

💭 Think about it

Who did you thank today? Is there someone you often forget to thank?
