The Kite String

🎈 Ages 4-8🎈 Ages 8-12🤝 Friends💛 Kindness & manners

⏱ 4 min readहिन्दी में पढ़ें

At the edge of the village there was a field so full of yellow mustard flowers that it looked like someone had spilled the sun across the ground. And every single afternoon, Meera and Chhotu came here to fly their kite.

It was a red and yellow kite with a long, wiggly tail, and the two of them had made it together. They were best friends. Everybody knew that.

"Look, Meera!" Chhotu shouted, holding his arms out to the breeze. "The wind is perfect today! Our kite will fly really, really high!"

"It's my turn first today," said Meera, reaching for the string. "Give it to me."

Chhotu pulled the spool back. "No way! I haven't even flown it yet!"

"You always go first. That's not fair!"

"I do not!"

"You do!"

Meera folded her arms. "Fine. I'm not talking to you."

"Fine!" said Chhotu. "Then I won't talk to you either!"

So they sat down, far apart, with their backs to each other and their faces as grumpy as two rain clouds. And the kite, which nobody was flying now, just lay quietly on the ground between them, its tail curled up sadly in the grass.

Then, whoooosh, a big gust of wind swept across the field.

The mustard flowers bent low. And over by the fence, the neighbour's tiny baby goat got such a fright that it bolted away, bleating, straight into a thorny bush. Its little legs got tangled. The more it wriggled, the more the thorns held on.

"Meh-eh-eh!" it cried.

Meera was on her feet in a flash. "Oh no! The poor thing is stuck!" She ran over and knelt down. "Don't be scared, little one. I'll get you out."

She grabbed the thorniest branch and lifted it. But now both her hands were full. "Oh... if I hold the branch, how do I lift the goat? I can't do it alone."

Behind her, a voice said quietly, "Meera, can I help?"

It was Chhotu. The grumpy cloud had gone from his face. "You hold the branch, and I'll lift the goat."

Meera didn't even stop to think. "Yes, Chhotu! But gently, so it doesn't get hurt."

Meera pushed the prickly branch up high, her arms trembling. Chhotu slid his hands carefully under the baby goat's soft tummy.

"One... two... three!"

Out it came! Not a single scratch. The baby goat shook its ears, gave a happy little skip, and went bouncing back home across the field.

Chhotu grinned. "See? We did it together!"

Meera looked at her scratched fingers, and then at her friend. "I'm sorry, Chhotu. I fought for nothing."

"I'm sorry too," said Chhotu. And then he held out the kite string. "You go first today."

"Well done, children!" They spun round. Dadi was standing at the edge of the field, smiling. "I saw it all. When you work together, even thorns can't stop you."

Meera took one end of the string, and gave Chhotu the other. "Dadi, now we'll fly our kite together too!"

And that afternoon, the red and yellow kite flew higher than it had ever flown before, up and up into the blue, because this time two pairs of hands were holding the string.

⭐ The lesson

Friendship is bigger than winning, and together we can do so much more.

💭 Think about it

Have you ever fought with a friend over whose turn it was? How did you make up?
