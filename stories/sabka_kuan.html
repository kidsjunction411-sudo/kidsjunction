Everyone's Well

🎈 Ages 4-8🎈 Ages 8-12🌳 Our environment🤝 Friends

⏱ 5 min readहिन्दी में पढ़ें

In the middle of our village, under a huge old banyan tree, stood a stone well. Long ago, everybody came here for water. Mothers filled their pots, farmers watered their oxen, and children leaned over the edge to shout "Hellooo!" and hear the well shout back.

But now the well was dry. Its pulley was broken. And it was full of rubbish: plastic bags, broken pots, old sandals and leaves, lots and lots of leaves.

"Chhotu, look," said Meera, peering over the edge from a safe distance. "There's so much rubbish in there."

Chhotu sighed. "That's why everyone has to walk all the way to the river for water. My mum goes twice a day."

Ramu Kaka was passing by with his cart. "Yes, my dears," he said, shaking his head. "Someone will fix the well one day."

That's what everyone said. Someone will fix it. One day. Somebody should really do something.

Meera put her hands on her hips. "Kaka, why can't we be that someone?"

Kaka blinked. Behind him, Dadi, who had been listening, began to smile. "What a good idea!" she said. "But remember one rule. Never, ever go inside the well. It is deep and it is dangerous."

"Promise, Dadi!" said Meera. "We'll only work from the outside."

Meera and Chhotu ran through the village, knocking on doors and calling names. Soon Anu, Golu and Kabir came running.

"I'll help too!" puffed Golu. "And afterwards, we'll all eat laddoos!"

Everyone laughed, and then they made a plan, because a big job is just a lot of small jobs standing in a line.

"Golu and I will clear all the rubbish around the well," said Chhotu.

Ramu Kaka rolled up his sleeves. He wasn't going to just talk anymore. "Stand back, children. I'll pull out the rubbish inside with the bucket."

"Yes, Kaka!" said Kabir. "And I'll empty every bucket into the sack."

Anu held up a little pot of blue paint. "And I'll paint flowers on the wall. Our well will look so pretty!"

So they worked. Bucket up, bucket emptied, bucket down again. Sweep, sweep, sweep. One blue flower, then two, then a whole garden of them growing around the stones.

But there was still one problem. The broken pulley. It hung high above the well, rusty and stuck. Who could fix that?

"The pulley is high up," said Dadi, "so Kaka will climb the ladder. You children hand him the oil and the new rope from below."

"Okay, Dadi," said Kabir. "We'll be careful."

Kaka climbed. The children passed things up. A few drops of oil, a twist of the rope, a squeak, a creak, and then...

"There you go, children!" called Kaka. "The pulley turns again!"

Everyone cheered so loudly that the birds flew out of the banyan tree.

By evening the well was shining. The stones were swept, the blue flowers glowed in the sunset, and the new rope hung ready.

There was just one thing missing. Golu leaned in, looked down, and his face fell. "All that work... and still no water!"

Nobody knew what to say. They went home tired, a little sad, wondering if it had all been for nothing.

And that very night, the sky rumbled. Lightning flashed. And the rain came pouring down, the first big rain of the year, drumming on the roofs and splashing in the lanes all night long.

In the morning, Chhotu was the first one there. He leaned over and his shout echoed back up at him. "Look! Look, everyone! Our well is filling up with water!"

Clean, clear rainwater, filling the clean well that they had cleaned together, because now there was no rubbish to spoil it.

Soon the whole village gathered round. "Well done, children," said Dadi, her eyes shining. "The whole village is proud of you."

Ramu Kaka scratched his head and laughed. "We grown-ups only talked about it. You actually did it."

Meera shook her head. "It's everyone's well, Kaka. And everyone fixed it together."

And then, of course, Golu got his laddoos.

⭐ The lesson

When we all lend a hand, even a big job becomes easy.

💭 Think about it

Is there something in your street or school that everyone says 'someone should fix'? What could you and your friends do about it?
